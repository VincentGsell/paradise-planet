Build a real-time tiny planet that runs in the browser: a living world you can fly
around, from orbit down to the grass. Meadows breathe in the wind, birds cross the sky,
clouds drift by and their shadows run over the hills, the sun crosses the day; and under
the waves, life goes on. It only needs to look beautiful and let me fly freely around it.

PRIORITIES (in this order when they conflict)
1. Visual quality around the camera
2. Stable frame pacing at the target spec
3. Small bundle size
Load time is not a priority: the planet may build itself while I fly, as long as what
surrounds the camera is detailed and stable.

CONSTRAINTS
- No Three.js/Babylon.js or other engines. WebGPU rendered.
- Everything procedural: no texture/model/sound files. Deliverable is a single
  self-contained index.html that runs from a double-click and from GitHub Pages.
  Report minified size.
- Zero per-frame JS allocations (no GC stutter). Preallocate all buffers.
- No jitter or z-fighting from orbit to 1 m above the grass (double precision on the CPU,
  camera-relative rendering, reversed-Z).
- The planet technology comes from my engine OpenG (Rust + WGSL): port the minimum that
  makes a planet live, not a web version of OpenG.

TARGET
- Windows 11 25H2, Chrome 157, RTX 5070 Ti, 2560×1440 at full resolution:
  GPU frame time ≤ 11 ms average, no frame > 16.7 ms during a scripted 60 s fly-through.
- RTX 5070, 3840×2160 at 60 Hz: locked 60 fps on all three detail levels
  (dynamic resolution allowed).

PLANET
- Diameter chosen in the start menu, from 10 to 50 km
- Ridged multi-octave relief on a cube-sphere, quadtree LOD with geomorphing, no seams
- A sea with FFT waves, shore foam, and a real view from below (Snell window, absorption)
- Physically based atmosphere from the ground to orbit, stars at night
- Volumetric clouds drifting with the wind (no weather), their shadows on land and sea
- A sun with a day/night cycle, and a small control to slow down, speed up or pause time
- God rays, above all at sunset

TWO BIOMES, chosen by altitude, slope and depth
- Above the water, the heart of the world: beaches, meadows of tall grass bending in the
  wind, trees, bushes, flowers and rocks; birds, gliding raptors, butterflies, pollen in
  the sunbeams, fireflies at dusk; light shafts through the clouds
- Under the water, from 20 to 50 m deep, as long as light reaches: a garden of kelp,
  coral and fish. Deeper, it fades to dark.
- Vegetation streams around the camera from a lattice anchored to the planet: the same
  plant in the same place, whatever the level of detail.

CONTROLS
WASD move (physical keys, so ZQSD on AZERTY), mouse look (pointer lock), Q/E down/up,
Shift to accelerate. Flight as in OpenG: the horizon levels itself near the ground.
Minimal UI: start menu (planet size, Low/Medium/High), time control, Esc menu, H stats.

VERIFICATION
- Visual critic: a separate subagent that sees ONLY your renders, never your code or
  reasoning. Give it 8 fixed camera poses (orbit, high altitude, cloud layer, beach,
  meadow at eye level, forest edge, shallow reef, garden at 40 m), rendered at
  2560×1440 in GPU-accelerated Chrome, plus a recording of a flight from orbit to the
  seabed to catch shimmer, aliasing, popping, seams and banding. It returns prioritized,
  concrete defects. Iterate until it reports nothing above "minor".
- Performance: GPU timestamp queries plus a frame-time log from a scripted 60 s flight
  (orbit → meadow → dive). Report average, 99th percentile and max.

DONE WHEN: both verification gates pass. Deliver index.html, the bundle size, the perf
report and the critic's final review.
